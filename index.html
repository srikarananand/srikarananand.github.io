FP&A · Cost-risk study

Where will the electricity budget break?

Problem
Multi-site operators budget electricity at last year's rate and do not know which states will break the budget.
What I did
Tested 4 budgeting rules across 51 states and 16 years, and built a 2027 base case from EIA's regional outlook.
Result
Last year's rate misses by more than 5% in 42% of state-years. Adding a flat 3% cuts that to 13%. I recommend at least 6.9% contingency, and 14–30% in HI, RI, DC, NV and CA.

Where it started: the EIA research assistant. The idea came from my Hamm Institute internship, and I built the assistant for an Oklahoma State course on agentic AI (April 2026). I then rebuilt it as a tested study because a budget decision needs a fixed answer a finance team can audit.

Policy analytics · Evidence check

Who protects ratepayers from data-center load?

Problem
State and utility plans say very different things about who pays for data-center growth, and they are too long to compare by hand.
What I did
Built a matrix of 11 public plans across 8 attributes. An automated check accepts a claim only if its quote appears word for word on the cited page.

Where it started: the state energy-plan assistant from my Hamm Institute internship. Limit: the check proves a quote exists, not that its label is right. Label accuracy is not measured.

Data science · Health survey

Language model plus gradient boosting

Problem
Each positive flag on a health survey triggers outreach, so false alarms cost effort.
What I did
Predicted frequent mental distress from the 2023 CDC BRFSS survey (433K respondents). Added a language-model score as one more input to a gradient boosting model. Trained on a 3,000-row sample.
Result
On a 750-row hold-out, precision rose from 40.6% to 48.4% (+7.8 points). Predictions are for opt-in outreach only, never for employment or insurance decisions.

Limit: a small hold-out means wide error bars. Treat the gain as promising, not proven.

National Champion · Purdue Data4Good Datathon

Optimizing TAPS support with automated journey mapping

Team of four: Srikaran Anand, Edgar Castillo, Bennett Frohock and Josias Hernandez Perez, M.S. Business Analytics and Data Science at Oklahoma State. Our entry, presented on a poster themed "LLMs for Good", won the national championship.

Challenge
TAPS supports military families grieving a loved one. Staff tracked each survivor's grief journey by hand, which was slow and could leave gaps in care.
What we built
A language model reads survivors' emails, calls and texts and answers TAPS's own survey. A Random Forest then places each survivor in one of six phases.
Result
Staff can reach out at the right moment, track less by hand, and spot who needs help sooner.

Why this design: survivors rarely fill in a survey, but they do write and call. A fixed rule cannot read grief or hope in free text, so the language model reads. The phase decision stays with a standard model trained on real survey answers.

Honest read of the results: the 0.94 score is for the survey-based phase classifier. When we hand-checked 30 log entries, the placed phase matched on 19 (63%), and most misses were one phase away.

Research · Co-authored paper, 2025

Accepted at three U.S. data conferences

Constraints-Based Perspective on Land Value Capture Strategies. Srikaran Anand and Rohith Satheesh Nair, M.S. Business Analytics and Data Science, Oklahoma State University.

Question
New transit raises nearby property values. Could Cook County reclaim part of that gain to pay for transit, and what stands in the way?
What we did
Joined parcel assessments with 25 years of CTA ridership, mapped parcels against rail stations, and fit a hedonic pricing model.
Conclusion
The uplift is real, but disconnected data is the bottleneck. We proposed open-data agreements and a lightweight predictive model.
15–20% highercertified values near CTA rail stations, in line with earlier transit studies
About $12.5K lessassessed value for each mile farther from a station, in the pricing model
8% of parcelssit in opportunity-zone tax exemptions, which limits how much value can be captured
Data is the bottleneckstatic parcel records against live ridership, missing station coordinates, and flood data on only 4% of parcels

Why it matters for your team: it is the same work as good BI. Join messy public datasets, model what drives value, and be clear about what the data cannot tell you yet.
