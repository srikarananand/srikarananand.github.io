03Featured win

Optimizing TAPS Support with Automated Journey Mapping

Team of four: Srikaran Anand, Edgar Castillo, Bennett Frohock and Josias Hernandez Perez, M.S. Business Analytics & Data Science at Oklahoma State. Our entry to the Purdue Data4Good Datathon, presented on an LLMs for Good themed poster, won the national championship.

  1. The challenge

    TAPS supports military families grieving a loved one. Staff tracked each survivor's grief journey by hand: slow work that risked gaps in care.

  2. What we built

    An LLM reads survivors' emails, calls and texts and answers TAPS's own survey; a Random Forest then places each survivor in a phase of grief.

  3. What it enabled

    Outreach at the right moment, less manual tracking, and no one falling through the cracks.

Why AI here: survivors don't fill in a survey every week, but they do write and call. No rule can score grief or hope from free text, so the LLM reads; the phase decision stays with a classic model trained on real survey responses.

24,707communication log records
2,950survivor survey responses
0.94weighted F1, phase classifier
23survey questions on grief, hope, faith and connectedness

How it works

From an inbox to a journey map
  1. Read every touchpoint

    Emails, calls and texts from the communication logs, with phase descriptions as context.

  2. LLM extracts signals

    Sentiment, keywords, resources mentioned and phase cues in each message.

  3. LLM answers the survey

    Ratings for inner strength and faith, emotional state and outlook, simulating survey responses.

  4. Random Forest classifies

    Trained on survey data with 9 selected variables and an engineered feature. Weighted F1 0.94.

  5. Journey map

    Each survivor's grief phase plotted over time, refreshed as new logs arrive.

A survivor's journey, at a glance

Staff see where someone is now and how they got there, so outreach can match the moment. The system updates with new data, runs regular quality checks and is refined with expert input.

04Research

Research accepted at three U.S. data conferences

Co-authored paper · 2025

Constraints-Based Perspective on Land Value Capture Strategies

Srikaran Anand and Rohith Satheesh Nair · M.S. Business Analytics & Data Science, Oklahoma State University

  1. The question

    New transit raises nearby property values. Could Cook County reclaim part of that gain to pay for transit, and what stands in the way?

  2. What we did

    Joined parcel assessments with 25 years of CTA ridership, mapped parcels against rail stations, and fit a hedonic pricing model.

  3. What it means

    The uplift is real, but disconnected data is the bottleneck. We proposed open-data agreements and a lightweight predictive model.

Key findings

  1. 15–20% highercertified values for properties near CTA rail stations, in line with earlier transit-uplift studies.
  2. About $12.5K lessassessed value for each mile farther from a station, in the hedonic model.
  3. 8% of parcelssit in opportunity-zone tax exemptions, which limits how much value can be captured.
  4. Data is the bottleneckStatic parcel records against live ridership, missing station coordinates and flood data on only 4% of parcels hide part of the uplift.
Geospatial analysisHedonic regressionPublic-sector open dataPolicy & equity analysis

Why it matters for your team: it's the same work as good BI. Join messy public datasets, model what drives value, and be clear about what the data can't tell you yet.

My first 90 days

Good analytics starts with understanding the people. I'd spend my first three months learning how your team works, then earn the right to change things.

  1. Days 1–30Listen and learn
    • Meet each teammate and stakeholder one-on-one.
    • Learn how decisions get made, who relies on which reports, and the unwritten rules.
    • Read the existing dashboards, data and docs before changing anything.
  2. Days 31–60Map and earn trust
    • Map stakeholders, workflows and data sources end to end.
    • Agree on the KPIs that matter and where each number comes from.
    • Find the friction: manual reports, slow handoffs, numbers that don't match.
    • Deliver one or two small wins the team asked for.
  3. Days 61–90Propose and build
    • Share what I learned and a short plan, agreed with my manager.
    • Start the highest-value BI or AI piece with the people who will use it.
    • Set a steady rhythm for feedback and iteration.
